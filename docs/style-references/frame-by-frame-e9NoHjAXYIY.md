# Frame-by-frame study: e9NoHjAXYIY ("Why Drivers Started Filming The Road", RicksClips3000)

Private study only; we never copy its story, characters or shots.

- **Material:** 1,865 frames (62.2 s, 30 fps, 720x1280), every frame read in 40-frame sheets.
- **Close reads:** big crops at frames 216-249, 330-352, 1125-1155, 1212-1230, 1520-1595, 1658-1700.
- **User notes added** (2026-11-12, after the full ayam render). Marked **[U]**.

## A. The core trick: 2D characters inside a real-looking 3D world
1. The characters are flat 2D drawings (thick ink outline, white face, dot eyes) but every background is a near-photoreal 3D render (cars, asphalt, grass, houses, pool).
2. The world sells "real"; the characters sell "cartoon". The mix is what feels high quality.
3. Characters always get a cast shadow on the ground in the direction of the scene's light (sitting man at 216-249: soft shadow on the asphalt toward camera-right).
4. A contact shadow (dark soft pool) sits right under the feet and under the seat when sitting; feet never float.
5. The character is colour-graded to the scene: blue-tinted at night (1212+), warm under string lights (1125), cool grey in rain (1658).
6. The background is lit by the same light the character seems lit by; light direction is consistent across a shot.
7. Depth of field: backgrounds behind a close character are softly blurred (1658 close-up: road and trees blurred, rain streaks sharp).
8. Foreground objects are sometimes blurred too (grass at 320-386 in front of the camera, out of focus).
9. Characters scale with perspective when they walk toward or away from the camera (walking man 330-352 is smaller than the woman in front).
10. Characters are composited at the right depth: they go behind car doors, behind steering wheels, behind the dashboard (1520-1600).
11. Characters stand IN water in the pool scene; below the waterline the legs are refracted (wobble, lighter tint) and cut by the water surface.
12. The duck toy floats and has its reflection in the water.
13. Lens-like effects: wet-windshield blur, raindrops on glass, wiper sweeps (1710-1770), dashcam text overlay (1658+).
14. Sun glare and bloom on bright surfaces (car paint highlights, white house walls, night lamps with halos).
15. String lights at night are real light sources: each bulb has a halo, the wall behind gets warm pools of light.
16. Night porch scene: a flashlight or porch light throws a bright ellipse on the pavement; characters standing in it are lit and cast long shadows out of it.
17. Car headlights at dusk (560-680) make lens streaks and light the road ahead.
18. Sky is never flat: gradients, clouds with lit tops, purple dusk, moody rain clouds.
19. Ground textures are rich: asphalt grain, road paint, grass blades, concrete joints, gravel.
20. Everything in the world has reflections where it should: glossy car paint shows sky and trees, wet road shows lights, pool shows the house.

## B. How a flat 2D character still looks 3D
21. Hands are dark spheres with a soft highlight and a darker underside: they read as balls, not dots (1658 close-up).
22. Limbs are dark (near-black) round tubes, not lines; at close range they have a subtle lighter edge on the lit side.
23. Hair has a gradient: darker at the outer edge, lighter on top where light hits.
24. Clothes have soft shading: a darker band on the side away from the light and on the lower half.
25. The yellow shirt at 1660 has faint fold stripes (vertical darker lines) for volume.
26. The face stays pure white (readable) but gets a faint cool shade at the bottom edge and at the side away from the light.
27. The head outline is thick; the body outline is a bit thinner; internal lines thinner still: line weight gives depth.
28. A thin bright rim appears on the lit edge at night (the porch-light rim on the head at 1212).
29. Accessories (sunglasses on the head, gold chain, sweat drop) have their own highlights (glint lines).
30. The sweat drop is light blue with a white highlight: a tiny 3D cue.
31. Sunglasses have a gradient lens and a gold frame with a highlight.
32. Characters turn: front, 3/4 and side views exist (driver at 1520 is in 3/4, walking man at 340 is in side view).
33. In 3/4 view the face features shift toward the facing side and the far eye is smaller.
34. When seated, the body bends (torso upright, thighs forward, knees bent) instead of a standing card.
35. The body leans into actions (leaning back on the car door at 216-249, slumping, leaning forward to point).
36. Overlap: an arm crosses in front of the body when pointing or holding something; the hand is drawn over the torso.
37. Held objects (briefcase, stamp sign, phone) are drawn at the hand, with the hand's fingers implied around the handle.
38. The shadow of the character also shows held objects (briefcase shape in the walking shadow).

## C. Walking and full-body motion [U]
39. Walking has a real cycle: legs scissor, the back foot lifts at the toe and swings forward, the front foot lands heel first (330-352).
40. One stride is about 8-10 frames; every frame has a different leg pose (drawn on ones).
41. The body bobs up at the passing pose and down at the contact pose.
42. The arms swing opposite to the legs; the arm holding a briefcase swings less and the case swings with a lag.
43. The head bobs with the body and tilts slightly with each step.
44. The walker also moves through space (across the frame), never walks in place.
45. Feet plant on the ground: no sliding; the planted foot stays still while the body moves over it.
46. Walking characters keep their cast shadow under them the whole time.
47. Turning to walk away: the character switches to the back or side view.
48. A character stopping settles: a small overshoot of the body, then still.
49. Idle characters are never frozen: breathing bob, small hand moves, head turns and blinks.
50. Gestures are big and clear: a hand raised high for a stamp sign "PAID" (340-386), arms crossed, palm up to explain, a facepalm.
51. Gestures arrive with anticipation (small dip) and settle with overshoot.
52. Sitting on the ground after a fall: legs spread forward, one knee up, the hand propping the body (240-300).
53. Stand-up and sit-down are animated through in-between poses, not swapped.
54. Driving: both hands on the wheel, the wheel turns, the body sways with the car (1520-1640).
55. Getting thrown: the body flies with stretched limbs and squash on landing.
56. A character can lean out of a car window; their arm hangs over the door.
57. Pointing: the arm extends straight, the index direction leads the eye to the subject.
58. Head turns happen over 2-4 frames with a blink to hide the swap.
59. Characters look at whoever is speaking; eyes follow movement.
60. Hands never hide behind the body for long; when they do, it is a motivated pose (hands in pockets, arms behind the back).

## D. Faces, mouths, teeth and tongue [U]
61. Open-mouth shapes show the top row of teeth (white band) when shouting or grinning (1145 "really weak": wide grin with teeth).
62. Wide-open shouting mouths show a pink or red tongue at the bottom of the mouth.
63. The mouth interior is dark (dark red-brown), not black; the tongue sits on it.
64. Small talking mouths switch every 2-3 frames between closed line, small o and medium open.
65. A smile is a curve; a big laugh is a D shape with teeth on top and the tongue at the bottom.
66. Grimace: a wide rectangle with teeth showing top and bottom.
67. Brows move with every expression (up for surprise, angled in for anger, tilted for worry).
68. Eyes squeeze into ">" "<" shapes at big laughs or pain (1145, 1155).
69. Surprise: eyes become big round circles with small pupils (1658 "It's a camera").
70. Worry: brows tilted up in the middle, a sweat drop appears at the temple.
71. Smug: half-closed eyelids and a side smile (stamp man at 340+).
72. Expressions swap on a blink or a head turn, and the face holds the new expression for a beat.
73. Mouth shapes match the syllables of the voice (lip-sync).
74. Face accessories react: sunglasses bounce on the head, a hat tilts on a head turn.
75. The head shape squashes slightly on a big mouth-open (the jaw drops).
76. Cheeks puff when chewing; the jaw moves up and down.
77. A stars or dizzy symbol appears after a hit; we use our own effects instead (sweat, steam, sparkles).

## E. Eating, food and props [U]
78. Biting: the hand brings the food to the mouth, the mouth opens wide (teeth visible), closes on the food, and a chunk disappears from the food.
79. Crumbs fly from the food and the mouth at each bite and fall to the ground with gravity.
80. Chewing: cheeks bulge, mouth moves in small circles, two or three chews per second.
81. Swallowing: a gulp with a small throat bump, then a satisfied face.
82. The eaten food changes shape step by step (bite marks), until only the bone is left.
83. Eggs crack in stages: a hairline crack, more cracks branch, a piece pops out, the shell splits in two halves, the content appears. [U]
84. A cracked shell leaves pieces on the ground.
85. Props are always picked up or put down by a hand; nothing appears or vanishes without an action.
86. Props have weight: heavy things pull the arm down, light things swing.
87. Props held up (the "PAID" stamp sign) move with the hand and arm, the sign text moves with the board, tilts with it and is never fixed in screen space. [U]
88. Signs have a thickness edge (cardboard or wood) and a shadow on the hand.
89. Signs wobble when waved: pivot at the hand, a little overshoot.
90. Money explosion (460-480): many bills, each a real 3D card tumbling, with motion blur.
91. Paper flying is affected by wind: drift, flutter, spin.
92. Smoke from a crashed car (940-965): soft grey, grows and drifts up, lit from the side.
93. Debris pieces on the ground after a crash stay there in the next shots (continuity).

## F. Light, shadow and reflection (detail) [U]
94. Every scene has a clear key light: the sun (day), a lamp (night), a flashlight (porch), headlights (dusk).
95. The key light direction is readable in the shadows on the ground.
96. Shadows are soft-edged (penumbra) but dark near the contact point.
97. Shadow colour is tinted by the sky (blue-violet in day, deep blue at night), never pure grey.
98. Fill light comes from the sky: shaded areas are blue, lit areas warm.
99. Bounce light: the red car tints the ground near it red; the grass tints the lower body of characters green.
100. Rim light: at night the lamp behind characters outlines the head and shoulders.
101. Specular highlights on glossy objects (car paint, chrome, glass, water) move as the camera moves.
102. Reflections: the wet road reflects headlights as long streaks; the pool reflects the house, lights and duck.
103. Glass reflects the sky (car windows look blue-white, not see-through).
104. At dusk the sky is purple-orange; the light on objects is warm on one side and violet on the other (two-colour lighting).
105. Night scenes are blue but never black; lamps make warm islands.
106. Light pools on the ground under lamps are elliptical with a soft falloff.
107. Light glows: lamp bulbs bloom, the halo extends beyond the bulb.
108. Volumetric hints: haze in the distance, light beams in dust (car interior sunlight at 1500).
109. Ambient occlusion: dark creases where objects meet the ground (car tyres, walls, pool edge).
110. Time of day changes between scenes give rhythm (day, dusk, night, rain).
111. Rain scenes: everything desaturated and cool, wet ground shiny, spray behind cars, drops on the lens.
112. Weather affects light: overcast means soft shadows; sun means hard shadows.
113. Interior shots: window light from one side, the dark interior, a bright exterior.
114. Exposure shifts: when the camera turns toward a bright window the interior darkens.
115. Light flicker on fire and warning lights (hazard light blink).

## G. Perspective and depth (detail) [U]
116. Strong perspective: low cameras near the ground make cars and people tower (80-145, 210-300).
117. Leading lines: road markings, curbs, fences lead toward the subject.
118. Foreground framing: grass, car doors, steering wheel, window frames in the foreground.
119. Three layers in nearly every shot: foreground prop, subject in the middle, a detailed background.
120. Horizon placement varies: low (sky-heavy) for drama, high for ground action.
121. Wide-angle close-ups distort slightly (the car front at 99-145 bulges).
122. Atmospheric perspective: far trees and buildings are lighter and bluer.
123. Size cues: other cars, houses and trees at a consistent scale.
124. The ground plane is always visible and textured, so depth reads.
125. Characters overlap each other at different depths (porch scene 1212: three characters at three depths).

## H. Camera: active, not aggressive [U]
126. In each scene the camera always moves slowly: a slow dolly in, a slow pan or a slow orbit.
127. Movement speed is gentle: it covers about 5-10 % of the frame width per second.
128. No sudden whips inside a scene; a fast move happens only when the story has an impact (crash).
129. Camera moves follow the subject: track alongside a moving car (560-830), follow a walking person.
130. Handheld-like micro shake only during impacts and in-car shots (car vibration).
131. Moves start and stop with easing (no linear starts).
132. Dashcam and in-car shots stay locked to the car and move with it.
133. Over-the-shoulder and POV shots put the viewer inside the action (steering wheel POV at 147-209).
134. Low tracking shots near the asphalt as cars pass.
135. Rack focus between foreground and background subjects.
136. A shot holds 2-5 s, then changes.
137. Framing keeps the subject in the thirds; the camera re-frames smoothly as the subject moves.
138. When a character speaks, the camera drifts slowly toward them.
139. After an impact the camera settles with a small overshoot.
140. Zoom punch on a punchline is small (about 5-8 % scale), not a big jump.

## I. Scene changes: fade or dissolve, then an active camera [U]
141. Scene changes use a quick cross-dissolve (36-39, 144-147, 305-313, 481-483, 556-559, 830-845, 1100-1101, 1160-1161, 1300-1310, 1500-1505, 1690-1700, 1800-1806).
142. Dissolve length: 3-12 frames; longer (10-15) when time or place changes a lot.
143. Some transitions dip through a darker frame (fade to black and back) when time passes.
144. Within a scene there are no hard jumps; the change of angle is motivated by a new beat.
145. A dissolve often goes between similar compositions (car in centre into car in centre).
146. A transition through an effect: the money explosion fills the frame and opens onto the next scene (460-483).
147. A wipe by a foreground object: the wiper blade or a car passing across the lens hides the cut (1710-1720).
148. After a dissolve the new shot is already moving (the camera never starts from rest at a cut).
149. Sound bridges carry over the cut.
150. Brightness is matched across dissolves to avoid flashes.

## J. Composition and staging
151. The subject is large in frame (characters fill 30-60 % of the height in close shots).
152. Two-shots: two characters at different depths, the speaker nearer.
153. Characters face each other when talking; profile or 3/4 view.
154. Background characters react (the green-shirt man at 216-300 reacts to the yellow man).
155. Contrasting colours separate subject and background (red car against blue sky and green grass).
156. Silhouettes are clear: limbs held away from the body in action poses.
157. Empty space in the direction the character looks.
158. Symmetric shots for confrontation (the car front, the boss at the desk 970-1100).
159. Props placed for storytelling: the desk with a name plate, a tie on the boss, family photos on the wall.
160. Background detail: skyscraper outside the office window, posters, plants, cabinets.

## K. Effects and particles
161. Smoke, dust, sparks and money each have their own shape language.
162. Particles have motion blur or streaks when fast.
163. Particles fade and shrink as they die.
164. Rain is visible across the whole frame and in front of and behind characters.
165. Raindrops hit the ground (small splashes) and the windshield (drops sliding down).
166. Wipers clear the water in arcs; the water returns.
167. Speed lines appear only in rare action moments.
168. Impact frames: a white flash or 2-frame hold at the hit.
169. Effects take the scene's light (smoke lit by the sun on one side).
170. Water spray behind car tyres on a wet road.

## L. Colour and grade
171. Saturated primaries for the subject (red car, yellow shirt), muted surroundings.
172. Grade per scene: warm day, purple dusk, blue night, grey rain.
173. Contrast is high: deep shadows plus bright highlights.
174. Blacks are not crushed: the shadow side keeps colour.
175. Characters' white faces are slightly tinted by the scene grade so they sit in the image.
176. Highlights bloom softly (white walls in sunlight).
177. Vignette: a slight darkening at the frame edges in night and in-car shots.
178. Skin and clothes keep their own colours under coloured light, but shift toward the light colour.

## M. Pace and storytelling
179. A new visual idea every 1-2 s; text captions per word (we do not use captions; the user adds them).
180. Each line of voice has a matching action on screen.
181. Setups pay off later (camera shows the empty road, then the crash is proved).
182. Reaction shots after every punchline (a 1-2 s face hold).
183. The climax uses the biggest effect (money explosion, smoke).
184. The ending loops back to the opening setting (car interior).

## N. What we take into "Satu Ayam yang Sama" (action list)
185. Fix: protest sign text must ride with the board (same rotation, tilt and sway as the board and hand). [U]
186. Characters get a real cast shadow from the sun or the main lamp (shadow proxy card turned to face the light), plus the contact blob.
187. Characters get light-side and shadow-side shading on the card: a cool multiply band on the side away from the light, a warm rim on the lit side, soft (blurred).
188. Night or bonfire scenes: the rim comes from the fire; day scenes: from the sun; sunset: a warm rim plus a violet shadow side.
189. Hands become shaded balls (darker underside, small highlight); limbs keep the bold black.
190. Open mouths show a top row of teeth; wide-open mouths also show a tongue; the mouth interior is dark red.
191. Eating: crumbs fly from the food and the mouth on each bite, fall with gravity, bounce once and fade; chewing keeps going between bites.
192. Eggs crack open in stages (crack lines, shell halves part, chick pops out), shell pieces stay on the ground.
193. Walk: a real stride (legs on ones, heel-toe), bob, arm swing, and the character moves through space with the shadow.
194. Camera: slow constant drift in every scene (active), gentler glides, smaller punches, no whip pans between scenes.
195. Scene changes: fade through black (or a dark dissolve) instead of tilting up to the sky.
196. Sunset protest: low warm sun with long shadows, a sun disc with glow, warm rim on characters and signs, violet shadows, wet puddles that reflect the sky and the people.
197. More reflections: puddles after the storm, the river or sawah water, glossy metal (coins, chain, car).
198. Bloom on light sources (sun, bonfire, lamps) and a light vignette.
199. Foreground layer (grass, posts, fence) blurred in front of the camera in some shots, for depth.
200. Keep the bold black limbs, white faces and plain stylised cast (our own look; we do not copy their characters).
