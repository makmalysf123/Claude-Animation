# "Why the Second Draw Always Wins" (LC8hYwUZ-kU): every frame (2026-11-12)

Private study.

- All 1,681 frames (56 s at 30 fps) were captured by seeking frame by frame and read in order as 43 sheets of 40 frames.
- Key moments were also read frame by frame at a larger size: the panic, the hat pop and fall, the pupil-to-window transition, the window fly-through, the walk with the barber pole, and close-ups of colour and light.

## B. Camera (36 findings)
1. One continuous move often carries a whole beat, for example following the bullet from the gun to the target (f24-89).
2. The camera follows a moving object to reach the next subject (the bullet, then the holster).
3. Tilting down to an insert and back up is a single move (f86-159, f430-455).
4. Pull-backs reveal the geography: wide, both men tiny (f140-169).
5. Pans pass one character to reach the other (f200-251), and the first one leaves the frame naturally.
6. Cranes go from the torso up to the face (f256-289).
7. Talking holds are never still: a slow push and a tiny sway (f290-409).
8. A whip tilt on an action word is 3-4 frames with motion blur (f430-433).
9. When something hits, the camera follows the body down (f504-530).
10. Low camera at the subject's level for the fallen man (f520-628).
11. Slow orbit around a still subject (f540-600).
12. Lateral tracking alongside a walker (f640-770).
13. Foreground objects pass between the camera and the subject (the barber pole and porch posts, f650-665) and give parallax.
14. A strong push-in from a medium two-shot to a close-up during dialogue (f1040-1080).
15. Pan along a table to introduce several people (f1106-1160).
16. Orbit round a table to an over-the-shoulder (f1215-1280).
17. **Push into an eye, then dissolve the pupil into a round window** (f1314-1344).
18. Pull back from that window into a new room, establishing the new place (f1344-1400).
19. Push into a window and **fly through it** to the outside (f1600-1627).
20. Dissolves are short (4-8 frames) and are used only for time or place changes: f252, f410, f630, f772, f936, f1102, f1532.
21. Hard cuts: almost none in 56 s.
22. Every dissolve joins two frames of similar composition (subject in the same spot), so the eye does not jump.
23. Dissolve into a time-of-day change in the same room (day → night, f1530-1536).
24. Camera roll is used on the fall and on the orbits.
25. The camera height changes all the time: ground (bullet), chest (talk), high (wide street), low (fallen man).
26. Depth of field: backgrounds blurred in close-ups (photography shop, saloon); wides sharp.
27. Strong foreground framing: porch posts, the barber pole, a shoulder.
28. The subject stays in a third of the frame and leaves room in the direction they look or move.
29. Moves ease in and out (no jerky starts).
30. A push into a face marks emphasis (f1280-1320).
31. Inserts of hands and props carry the story points (holster, cards, telegraph).
32. The camera follows a body or an object, never an empty point.
33. Shots of different places are joined by motion (bullet, eye, window), not by jumps.
34. The bullet shot runs at slow motion on top of the camera move (it spins visibly).
35. Wide shots are short (about 1 s); close-ups and talks are longer.
36. The end is a tableau (a standoff) with a slow push.

## C. Characters: face, head, hat (22 findings)
1. The mouth changes shape almost every frame while talking: dash, open D with teeth, O, gritted teeth, smirk.
2. Brows move on the words: up on questions, V on anger, one raised on doubt.
3. Eyes: lidded deadpan by default; dots when looking; **rings with tiny pupils** in shock (f412-428, f1314); X or spiral when knocked out.
4. Swapping eye type is instant, but the head shape follows (wider in shock).
5. **The head grows in shock** (f412-420), together with a push-in.
6. **The hat pops off and flies away** when he is shot (f504-530), spinning.
7. Hats sit low on the head (brim over the brow), tilt with the head, and have a band and shading.
8. Sweat drop on the nervous man (f1160).
9. Sand on the face after the fall: **the skin gets a dirt texture** (f520-628).
10. Faces are warm cream, not pure white. They pick up the scene light: orange at sunset, blue-ish at night, warm under lamps.
11. The face has a soft gradient: the lit side is brighter, a darker crescent sits on the far side.
12. Moustaches and beards are separate drawn shapes.
13. A yellow flame aura means panic (f418-505).
14. A white sparkle ring on a quick grab (f436).
15. Talking characters have small head bobs and turns.
16. Two characters talking face each other in 3/4 (f940-1100).
17. Over-the-shoulder shows the back of a head and hat (f1215-1280).
18. **All the characters in this video are men.** The cast of a western is male, and the extras are men too.
19. Each character has a clear silhouette: hat shape, clothes colour, moustache.
20. Expressions are exaggerated at story peaks and calm in between.
21. Blinks are quick and frequent during holds.
22. The eye line always points at the other character or the object.

## D. Characters: body, arms, legs (16 findings)
1. **Arms and legs are near-black strokes** (dark plum-black), whatever the clothes colour.
2. **Hands are solid black round dots,** clearly visible against everything.
3. **Feet are solid black boot shapes,** small and pointed, clearly read.
4. **No triangles or flaps at the feet:** the clothes end cleanly at the torso or hem.
5. Arms bend with real poses: aiming the rifle with both arms forward (f0-19), the hand on the holster grip, a hand carrying a mug.
6. The walk bobs, the legs swing, and the body leans forward a little (f640-770).
7. The fall is a full-body tilt from standing to lying over about 14 frames, with a bounce on impact.
8. Lying flat: the body is really on the ground in perspective (a 3D placement, not a standing card).
9. Riders sit on horses and the horses walk (f770-830).
10. Seated players at the table: the bodies are hidden by the table, with only the heads and arms working.
11. The torso is a bean with a gradient (lit side to shade side) and a rim light on the shadow edge in golden light.

13. Props in the hands are 3D objects with their own shading (rifle, mug, cards).
14. Bodies are never perfectly symmetric: one arm does something, the other hangs.
15. The idle pose shifts weight; the head tilts.
16. Contact shadows sit under every foot (blue-violet blobs on the sand).

10. Props are period-specific (holsters, cards, telegraph); the world tells the time and place.
11. Wide shots show depth layers: foreground prop, subject, middle buildings, far hills.

## G. Effects and timing (10 findings)
1. Muzzle flash: orange cone plus a white flash, 2-3 frames, then smoke.
2. Bullet: spinning, with an orange-white trail and streaks.
3. Sparkle ring on a fast grab.
4. Panic aura: flickering yellow flame tongues around the body.
5. Dust burst when a body lands; sand flies in clumps.
6. Hat pop: it flies up and spins away.
7. Motion blur on whip moves.
8. Actions land on the narration words.
9. Holds of 2-4 s for talks; actions of 0.5-1 s.
10. Each place lasts 3-10 s, then a motivated transition.

## H. What our test still gets wrong (from the user's notes plus this study)
1. **Story continuity:** after the whirlwind the houses are gone. The bonfire and egg scenes must show ruins (rubble, bare plots, broken walls), not intact houses.
2. **Ink lines show through the storm fog** (tree outlines behind the funnel). The ink must fade with the fog, or far objects must be removed.
3. Rain still has gaps in places. The streaks must fill every part of the frame.
4. Expressions, head morph and hat behaviour (hat pop, hat tilt) were studied but not used enough. Use them at every story peak.
5. Light: no sun shafts, no window pools, no coloured darks. Add more light sources and two-colour lighting.
6. Transitions between very different places must be motivated: follow an object, match a shape, fly through an opening, or dissolve on matched compositions.
7. Objects must not be flat single colours: give them gradients, texture and trim.
8. Arms and legs fully black, hands black dots, black boots.
9. More men in the cast (and male extras).
10. Background life: moving props and extras in the background.
